Executive overview

5 things to know

• TroFuse-005 improved PFS and OS after platinum and immunotherapy in endometrial cancer, with more grade ≥3 AEs.

• KEYNOTE-C93 supports a chemotherapy-free strategy in dMMR endometrial cancer against chemotherapy alone; the comparison with chemoimmunotherapy remains unanswered.

• VOLGA and DeLLphi-305 provide OS evidence; DASL-HiCaP adds a significant conventional-imaging MFS benefit in very high-risk prostate cancer, with OS still unproven.

• Krascendo 1 and SAFFRON strengthen mechanism-selected lung cancer treatment; population and assay definitions matter.

• Negative trials and statistical qualifications change interpretation: non-significant OS, descriptive analyses and failed non-inferiority cannot be presented as confirmatory successes.

Gynaecological cancers

TroFuse-005 | sacituzumab tirumotecan after platinum and immunotherapy →

Randomised phase 3; 776 assigned (386/390), with safety populations of 384/367. Median follow-up 14.0 months. PFS: 7.2 months (95% CI 5.6–7.6) versus 3.9 (3.7–5.3); HR 0.59 (0.50–0.70), P<0.0001. OS: 19.0 months (16.1–not reached) versus 12.1 (11.0–15.2); HR 0.68 (0.55–0.84), P=0.0001. Grade ≥3 all-cause AEs: 69% versus 57%; serious AEs: 35% versus 32%; treatment-related deaths: three in each arm. Randomisation incorporated MMR and TROP2 expression.

KEYNOTE-C93 | first-line pembrolizumab alone in dMMR endometrial cancer →

Phase 3, open-label; 299 assigned (149/150). BICR PFS: 38.2 months (95% CI 17.9–not reached) versus 8.4 (6.8–10.4); HR 0.57 (0.42–0.79), P=0.0002. Gynaecological cancers

BEHOLD-1 | mocertatug rezetecan in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer →

Mo-Rez is a B7-H4-directed ADC carrying a TOP1 inhibitor. In the open-label phase 1b dose-selection component, 136 patients were randomised to 2.8, 4.8 or 5.8 mg/kg every 3 weeks; median prior lines were two (range 1–5). At the 30 June 2026 cutoff, median follow-up was 13.4 months in each group. The primary endpoint was investigator-assessed confirmed ORR per RECIST 1.1.

REJOICE-Ovarian02 | raludotatug deruxtecan plus bevacizumab in PROC →

Cohort B-1 of this phase 1b/2 study evaluated the CDH6-directed TOP1-inhibitor ADC R-DXd plus bevacizumab 15 mg/kg every three weeks. Patients had high-grade serous ovarian, primary peritoneal or fallopian tube cancer, measurable disease, one to three prior lines and radiographic progression <6 months after platinum; primary platinum-refractory disease was excluded. At 3 February 2026, 30 patients had been treated: ten at R-DXd 4.8 mg/kg and 20 at 5.6 mg/kg. Median follow-up was 4.5 months (range 0.8–8.8). Primary endpoints were safety; BICR ORR was secondary.

Gynaecological cancers

FONTANA | torvutatug samrotecan in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer →

Torvu-sam (AZD5335) is an FRα-directed ADC with a topoisomerase-1 inhibitor payload. This phase 1/2a, open-label Module 1 analysis included dose escalation (M1A; FRα-unselected, unrestricted prior lines) and expansion (M1B; ≤3 prior lines). Dose optimisation required ≥25% tumour cells with ≥1+ staining; additional expansion enrolled <25%. At 16 March 2026, 223 patients had been treated: 71 M1A, 124 M1B1 and 28 M1B2; median exposure was 7.1 months. The primary objective was safety/tolerability.

Gynaecological cancers

DUO-E | interim OS and safety update →

At the 2 February 2026 cutoff, interim OS maturity was 52%; median follow-up among censored patients was 52 months. The three arms were carboplatin/paclitaxel (CP; n=241), CP plus durvalumab followed by durvalumab (CP+D; n=238), and CP+D followed by durvalumab plus olaparib (CP+D+O; n=239). Median OS was 39.6, 40.7 and 51.1 months, respectively. Versus CP, OS HRs were 0.87 (95% CI 0.68–1.11; P=0.259) for CP+D and 0.77 (0.60–0.98; P=0.037) for CP+D+O. Neither comparison met the prespecified statistical significance criterion according to LBA37.

Exploratory dMMR OS HRs were 0.57 (0.30–1.05) and 0.43 (0.21–0.83); in pMMR disease, 0.95 (0.73–1.24) and 0.85 (0.65–1.11), respectively. These estimates do not establish a subgroup interaction. The abstract provides no numerical updated severe/fatal safety rates. Its CP+D+O OS event row lists 115/239 alongside 48.7%, an internal arithmetic inconsistency; the event count is retained without adopting that percentage.

Clinical interpretation The numerical OS difference does not establish a confirmatory OS benefit. The full SAP and current manuscript are needed to resolve alpha allocation and the table inconsistency.

Gynaecological cancers

BEATcc | final overall survival in cervical cancer →

BEATcc randomised 410 patients with metastatic, persistent or recurrent cervical cancer to chemotherapy and bevacizumab with or without atezolizumab. At final analysis, 277 deaths had occurred; median follow-up was 57.2 months (95% CI 54.9–59.5). Median OS was 31.9 versus 22.7 months; HR 0.69 (95% CI 0.54–0.88). Four-year OS was 40% versus 26%. Median PFS2 was 23.3 versus 17.0 months; HR 0.62 (0.49–0.78).

Exploratory OS HRs were 0.71 (0.50–0.99) for PD-L1 CPS ≥1 and 0.53 (0.32–0.88) for CPS <1. No interaction test is supplied. In the control arm, 64/110 patients receiving post-progression therapy received immunotherapy. No patients remained on study treatment; the abstract describes late safety as consistent with previous reports without numerical updated severe/fatal rates.

Clinical interpretation The final analysis supports a sustained survival benefit. Subgroup point estimates alone cannot establish equal benefit across PD-L1 strata. Indication-level EU/Greek access remains a separate implementation check.

Gynaecological cancers

Other ovarian and cervical ADC presentations →

ZW191 (LBA40). Eighty patients received 6.4 mg/kg (n=37) or 9.6 mg/kg (n=43), with a median four prior lines and no FRα selection. Excluding clear-cell histology, response-evaluable populations were 31 and 38: ORR was 58% at each dose, but confirmed ORR was 52% and 45% (48% pooled). Including clear-cell cases, confirmed ORR was 47% and 41% (44% pooled; n=78). Median DOR was not reached; PFS was not provided. Grade ≥3 neutropenia was 8% versus 23%, anaemia 8% versus 14%, and pleural effusion 5% versus 9%.

JSKN033 (1243RO). Among 64 enrolled patients with pretreated cervical cancer, efficacy denominators were 34 at 8.4 mg and 28 at 6.7 mg, across schedules. ORR was 18/34 (52.9%) versus 5/28 (17.9%); at 8.4 mg, DCR was 88.2% and median PFS 7.2 months. The abstract does not explain why efficacy denominators total 62. Prior immunotherapy, bevacizumab and ADC exposure occurred in 75%, 56.3% and 10.9%; HER2 IHC was 0 in 71.9%. Grade ≥3 TRAEs occurred in 26.6%; injection reactions in 95.3% (grade ≥3, 1.6%); ILD in one patient. No treatment-related dose reduction, discontinuation or death was reported.

Clinical interpretation Small, noncomparative dose cohorts do not support a cross-ADC efficacy ranking. Confirmed response, histology and efficacy denominators must remain explicit.

Original ESMO 1243RO, cervical cancer session.

VOLGA | perioperative durvalumab and enfortumab vedotin →

Phase 3 in cisplatin-ineligible/refusing MIBC; durvalumab–EV n=233, durvalumab–tremelimumab–EV n=233, control n=229. Doublet versus control: 24-month EFS 63.5% (95% CI 56.5–69.7) versus 45.1% (38.2–51.8); HR 0.51 (0.38–0.67), P<0.001. OS at 24 months: 78.4% versus 69.4%; HR 0.58 (0.40–0.83), P=0.003. Triplet EFS at 24 months: 60.1%; HR versus control 0.61 (0.47–0.80), P<0.001. Grade ≥3 AEs: 71.1%, 79.5%, 50.2%; grade ≥3 TRAEs in the experimental arms: 30.7%, 44.5%.

MOIO | reduced-frequency checkpoint inhibition →

MOIO planned 646 patients and randomised 340; the modified ITT interim population comprised 308. After response to six months of checkpoint inhibition, patients continued the same dose at standard frequency or every three months. Median follow-up was 18.6 months; the non-inferiority margin was HR 1.3. Median PFS was 25.0 months (95% CI 19.4–NE) with standard dosing versus 21.9 (12.1–31.6) with reduced frequency; reduced-versus-standard HR 1.35 (0.90–2.00), reported P=0.0635. OS HR was 2.0 (1.1–3.9), P=0.0143. Grade 3–4 immune-related AEs were 10.5% versus 9.5%; serious AEs 26.3% versus 25.0% (standard versus reduced).

Clinical interpretation Non-inferiority was not demonstrated; the mortality signal and early-stopping recommendation argue against routine three-monthly dosing in this population. Formal PFS inferiority is not established by these estimates.

Statistical qualification: the abstract methods describe a one-sided futility rule at P<0.0192, whereas its results call P=0.0635 beyond the futility boundary. This inconsistency requires the SAP/IDMC decision record; the abstract alone does not resolve it. A numerical fatal immune-toxicity rate is not supplied.
