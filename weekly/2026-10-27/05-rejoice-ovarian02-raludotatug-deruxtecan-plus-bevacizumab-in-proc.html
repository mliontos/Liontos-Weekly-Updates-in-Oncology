Full Evidence Review
Early

Cohort B-1 of this phase 1b/2 study evaluated the CDH6-directed TOP1-inhibitor ADC R-DXd plus bevacizumab 15 mg/kg every three weeks. Patients had high-grade serous ovarian, primary peritoneal or fallopian tube cancer, measurable disease, one to three prior lines and radiographic progression <6 months after platinum; primary platinum-refractory disease was excluded. At 3 February 2026, 30 patients had been treated: ten at R-DXd 4.8 mg/kg and 20 at 5.6 mg/kg. Median follow-up was 4.5 months (range 0.8–8.8). Primary endpoints were safety; BICR ORR was secondary.

No DLTs were reported. One patient at 4.8 mg/kg had drug-related grade 1 ILD. At 5.6 mg/kg, a death classified by the investigator as grade 5 aspiration pneumonia and not drug related was subsequently adjudicated as drug-related ILD. The investigator TRAE table therefore does not capture the adjudicated fatal pulmonary toxicity. Dose reductions were for grade 3 anaemia and fatigue (one each); discontinuation events included grade 2 proteinuria at 4.8 mg/kg and grade 2 gastrointestinal fistula at 5.6 mg/kg. The source table prints any TRAEs at 4.8 mg/kg as “10 (10)”, an internally inconsistent count/percentage; no corrected percentage is asserted here.

Clinical interpretation Small cohorts and short follow-up support only preliminary activity. The dose groups cannot establish comparative efficacy or a preferred dose, especially with differing efficacy denominators. The fatal event adjudicated as drug-related ILD must remain prominent even though no DLTs and no investigator-attributed fatal TRAEs were reported. Absence of grade ≥3 AEs among ten lower-dose patients is not evidence that severe toxicity cannot occur.
